## 1. Realism audit — what changed and why

| # | Realism question | Finding | Action |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | The benchmark used a bladder fill constant τ_b = 0.15–0.5 s. Do real m³-scale floats inflate that fast? | **No.** Helicopter floats are inflated with nitrogen in 2–3 s [SRC md600n1997]. A CH-46 blow-down float took 13–14 s to full form against a 7 s design target, and a cool-gas generator reached 1 psig in 4 s [SRC reilly1981]. That is τ_b ≈ 0.7–4.5 s. | New sweep τ_b ∈ {0.3, 1, 2, 4, 6} s (n = 40 per vehicle, seeds 6000+). |
| R2 | Do the conclusions survive realistic fill times? | **Yes.** B3r median EFT changes by ≤ 1.8 s, B2 by ≤ 2.2 s. Success is ≥ 95 % for every strategy and every τ_b. FREE loses up to 13 s (ICE, τ_b = 6 s) because its guard predicts with the commanded rather than the realised volume. Reason: gas is released at detection while the car still floats on trapped air (40 s ICE, 21 s EV). | New Results subsection VII-H and Fig. 13. The ranking B3r ≥ FREE > B2 is unchanged. |
| R3 | Are airbag-type pyrotechnic inflators a realistic gas source? | **No.** Airbag cushions are more than an order of magnitude smaller than 1.4–2.2 m³. Stored N₂ at about 300 bar with a pyrotechnic valve — the helicopter practice — is realistic: 7.5 L (ICE) or 9.7 L (EV) of cylinder capacity. | Architecture (Fig. 2) and caption changed from "gas generators" to "N₂ inflation, stored 300 bar, pyro valve per station"; Discussion VIII-E added. |
| R4 | Window motors after immersion | Euro NCAP requires operation for ≥ 2 min [SRC euroncap2025], but the 12 V net may short. | "Backup supply ≥ 2 min" added to the architecture. |
| R5 | Unintended deployment at speed | This is the dominant hazard: bladders deployed on the road would affect braking and steering. | Firing path requires D1 + D2 + a vehicle-speed plausibility check (VIII-E). ASIL is still open. |
| R6 | Crash compatibility | Corner packs sit in the crumple zones. | Pressure vessels go central, outside the crush zones; corner packs must be crash-insensitive (VIII-B, VIII-E). |
| R7 | Hardware complexity of FREE | FREE needs an individually commanded valve per station. B3r needs only a fixed orifice ratio. | Recorded as a further argument for the one-shot design. |
| R8 | Moving water | Added buoyancy lifts the car off the road sooner and helps the current carry it. | Kept the inhibit branch: in moving water only windows and an alert are used (unchanged; now justified with flood statistics). |

**Interpretation.** The system weighs less than one extra passenger (75 kg). Its effect on dynamics is about 1 % of yaw
inertia, but only if the cylinders are packaged centrally. The design decision is therefore **central N₂ storage piped
to the corner bladders**.

## 3. What safety it gains

- **Success** (EFT ≥ T_req), from windows only (B0) to B3r: ICE 8 % → 99 %, EV 0 % → 97 % [SIM, benchmark draws].
- **Median escape window:** ICE 11 s → 165 s, EV 4 s → 104 s [SIM].

**Benefit chain.** Lives saved per equipped vehicle = r · φ_s · φ_u · φ_e · ΔP · A, where:

| Factor | Meaning | Value | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| r | submersion deaths per vehicle-year | see section 4 | SRC |
| φ_s | share of deaths in still or slow water | 0.3–0.7 | ASSUMED |
| φ_u | share not incapacitated by the crash | 0.45–0.70 | anchored on 57 % minor/no injury [SRC wintemute1990] |
| φ_e | share who actually leave when time suffices | 0.5–0.9 | ASSUMED |
| ΔP | success gain over windows only | 0.83–1.0 | SIM |
| A | discounted vehicle life | 11.9 | ASSUMED: 15 years, 3 % |

**Result: about 18 % of submersion deaths in equipped vehicles are averted (90 % interval 10–29 %).**

## 4. Cost per life saved

| Exposure | Deaths per million vehicle-years | Cost per life saved (median, 90 % interval) | Break-even system cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Netherlands: > 50 deaths/yr [SRC swov], 9.3 M cars [SRC cbs2025] | 5.4 | $63 M ($26–137 M) | $161 (US VSL) / $54 (EU VSL) |
| North America: ≈ 400 deaths/yr [SRC mcdonald2013], 298 M vehicles [SRC fhwa2026] | ≤ 1.3 (upper bound) | $254 M ($103–550 M) | $40 / $13 |

Reference values of a statistical life:
- US DOT VSL: $14.2 M, base year 2025 [SRC dotvsl2025].
- EU VSL: €4.3 M (MAIS 6 value [SRC eurocontrol_si]), about $4.7 M at an assumed 1.10 USD/EUR.

Exposure at which the cost per life saved equals the VSL:

| VSL | at $300 | at $600 | at $1,200 |
|---|---|---|---|
| US | 10 | 20 | 40 |
| EU | 30 | 60 | 120 |

(Deaths per million vehicle-years.)

**Honest verdict.**
- **Fitting flotation to every car is not cost-effective at today's national incidence.** This holds even in the
  Netherlands, and over the whole 90 % band.
- **Flotation pays in fleets with about 4–11× the Dutch exposure at $600 (2–22× over $300–1,200).**
- **Automatic windows**, which cost almost nothing on cars that already have power windows, are justified everywhere.
  This is consistent with the Euro NCAP incentive.

This result is now in the paper (Section VIII-C, Fig. 14) and in the abstract and conclusion.

## 5. Where it matters most (paper Table 7)

| Setting | Evidence | Water | Fit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canal and polder countries (Netherlands; likely also other delta and canal regions) | > 50 deaths/yr, 8.5 % of road deaths [SRC swov] | still | Highest national exposure documented. Still below break-even for all cars. |
| **Fleets working next to water**: ports and docks, ferry ramps, dike and canal maintenance, dredging, flooded quarry and mine pits, ice roads, police and rescue | No aggregate data found — REQUIRES SOURCE VERIFICATION. This is the first step of any business case. | still, ice | **Best case.** Pilot target. |
| North America | ≈ 400 deaths/yr [SRC mcdonald2013] | mixed | Low per vehicle. Windows first. |
| Flash floods (US, Australia, Spain) | > 50 % of US flood deaths are vehicle-related [SRC nws_mkx]; ≥ 81 Australian deaths in 20 yr, 43 % of flood deaths [SRC gissing2015] | moving | **Inhibit flotation.** Use detection, windows and alert. |
| Military tactical vehicles | 123 training deaths FY2010–2019; rollover in 63 % of fatal accidents [SRC gao21361]; Fort Hood 2016, LMTV overturned in a flooded low-water crossing, 9 deaths [SRC armytimes2016] | moving | The EFT metric (hatch/window egress), moving-water detection and egress HMI transfer. Flotation would need tonne-scale gas (Proposition 2 scales with mass). |
| Amphibious vehicles (AAV sinking 2020, 9 deaths [SRC usni2021]) | — | sea | A different problem: reserve buoyancy of a vehicle designed to swim. Out of scope. |
| Buses on canal roads (e.g. India) | ≥ 37 deaths in one event [SRC euronews2021]; no national statistic found | still, slow | High consequence, but about 10× the gas of a car. Exits that stay above water matter more than flotation. |

**Industry relevance.**
- **OEMs:** the result supports automatic windows with an EFT-based check now, and flotation as a fleet or regional option.
- **Fleet operators and insurers:** flotation is a measure for vehicles working next to water.
- **Defence:** the metric and the moving-water logic transfer; vehicle-scale flotation does not.
